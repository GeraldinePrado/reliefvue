Foundation

Help first. Explain clearly.

For households

A welcoming route to understand eligibility, prepare a household profile and follow a request. Thai and English should feel equally considered.

For donors

Make relief contributions and separate operating support understandable. Show where money moves without exposing private household information.

For reviewers

Readable evidence, clear reasons and human judgment. A calm interface should make uncertainty visible rather than hide it behind an AI score.

The proposed service combines a prepared SOL reserve, documented disaster evidence, human approval and one fixed grant per eligible household sent directly to a verified wallet. Personal information stays off-chain; public payout records and fund movements support transparent accounting. Its identity should express compassion and accountable decisions.

The intended allocation is benchmarked to three days of Thai essentials for a reference household of 3–5. The amount remains unvalidated. No response-time or purchasing-power promise belongs in the brand.

Voice

Compassion without promises.

Speak plainly

“See how household relief would work.”

“Your demo request needs more information.”

“This is a sample payout. No funds were transferred.”

Explain the next step, the reason and the person who can help. Use the same clarity for donors and households.

Keep claims grounded

Describe intended household grants, human review and public accounting. Avoid “instant aid,” “AI guarantees,” “corruption-proof,” “fully anonymous” or invented impact totals.

Cover line and application copy are proposed brand expressions, not approved performance claims.

Photography & composition

Real people. Real context.
Room to breathe.

Documentary photography should show agency, care and community. Follow the human emphasis you liked in GoFundMe, with ReliefVue’s own identity.

Look for

Prioritize photographs documented as taken in Thailand, with Asian communities and local everyday settings. Represent Thailand’s diverse residents without treating nationality or eligibility as something to infer from appearance. Natural light, recognizable context, eye-level framing and people making decisions or helping each other. Preserve skin tones. Use one strong image and quiet surrounding space.

Future field photography needs consent and accurate captions. Crop without removing context or covering faces with interface text.

Leave out

Cartoon people, synthetic disaster victims, distress used as decoration, stereotyped poverty, aggressive purple filters and crypto coins floating over humanitarian scenes.

Do not expose household documents, exact homes or names in public images. Do not present stock subjects as beneficiaries.

Current direction: real Chiang Mai flood photography from 2024–2026, covering flood impact and people helping. The three actual photographs above are remotely displayed from Thai Village for this local reference review. Public reuse permission is not confirmed; do not carry these assets into deployment until cleared. Other researched sources remain in the imagery register. Keep verified captions, credits and dates with selected photographs. Never imply these are ReliefVue operations or an active emergency.

Prepare before disaster.
Trace every relief payment.

ReliefVue is a proposed Solana-powered disaster relief platform designed to hold emergency funds before disasters strike. After human review and approval, eligible household grants would go directly to verified wallets.

Personal information would stay off-chain. Public payout records and fund flows on Solana would make distributions easier to follow, without publishing household identities.

The aim is to reduce unnecessary intermediaries and improve accountability. Lower costs, fewer delays and reduced misuse are goals to validate, not guaranteed results.

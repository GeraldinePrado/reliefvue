Foundation

Help first. Explain clearly.

For households

A welcoming route to understand eligibility, prepare a household profile and follow a request. Thai and English should feel equally considered.

For donors

Make relief contributions and separate operating support understandable. Show where money moves without exposing private household information.

For reviewers

Readable evidence, clear reasons and human judgment. A calm interface should make uncertainty visible rather than hide it behind an AI score.

The proposed service combines a prepared SOL reserve, documented disaster evidence, human approval and one fixed grant per eligible household sent directly to a verified wallet. Personal information stays off-chain; public payout records and fund movements support transparent accounting. Its identity should express compassion and accountable decisions.

Executive leadership sets a response budget and one fixed grant for that response. Reviewers approve routine eligible applications; supervisors handle exceptions. The 0.8 SOL grant is an illustrative value, not a validated emergency-needs benchmark. No response-time or purchasing-power promise belongs in the brand.

Voice

Compassion without promises.

Speak plainly

“Help should meet each household’s needs.”

“Your demo request needs more information.”

“This is a sample payout. No funds were transferred.”

Explain the next step, the reason and the person who can help. Use the same clarity for donors and households.

Keep claims grounded

Describe intended household grants, human review and public accounting. Avoid “instant aid,” “AI guarantees,” “corruption-proof,” “fully anonymous” or invented impact totals.

The hero and household-needs language reflect the current app. Use present-tense mission statements, and label demonstrations separately. Do not describe simulated totals as traction.

Imagery & composition

Human dignity.
Evidence with context.

Use authentic, consented photographs of community support and recovery when cleared images are available. A photograph must never imply that a person received ReliefVue support.

Documentary standard

Natural light, local context, accurate captions and photographer credits. Preserve skin tones and dignity. Never expose names, identity documents or exact homes.

No AI-generated people or disaster scenes should be used as evidence. External photographs in v03 were reference-only and have not been cleared for public reuse.

Current app limitation

The app still contains labelled illustrative Chiang Mai and household concept scenes. These are not documentary evidence and conflict with the earlier preference for authentic photography.

Before using those scenes in the pitch, replace them with cleared photographs or omit them from the presentation. Use the working map, grant flow and accounting screens as the demonstration evidence.

Ledger to household

Donors→Relief reserve→Household grants

Use clean diagrams, approximate area maps and labelled receipts. On-chain records demonstrate transfers; they do not prove real-world disaster impact or what a household purchased.

Motion & responsive behavior

Guide attention.
Keep the facts readable.

Public pages

Vertical single-line news carousel; gentle amber alert pulse. The hero uses a curved pencil-like gradient underline. Impact numbers count up, pause at their target and repeat. Fund-flow lights follow the arrows.

Respect reduced motion and stop nonessential animation when offscreen. Provide a keyboard path for scrolling timelines and lists.

Operations

Calm white analytics surfaces, navy text and purple selection. Use labelled charts with accessible values and tables. Avoid decorative number animation in balances or evidence decisions.

Stack map/list and analytics cards on narrow screens. Keep primary actions and form labels visible; do not hide meaning inside hover-only tooltips.

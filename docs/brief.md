# ReliefVue — ideathon product brief

Status: working brief from Geraldine's brainstorm, 2026-10-03. Product decisions below are intended direction, not claims that the public prototype implements them. This brief supplements the [recovery brief](recovery_brief_v02.md); code and deployment evidence determine what is demonstrable.

## Pitch in one paragraph

ReliefVue is a Thailand-first, Solana-based emergency reserve funded before a disaster. When a national or local authority publishes an official affected-area announcement, ReliefVue's founder reviews the original source and approves a precisely defined event, one household grant benchmarked to Thai emergency-essential prices, and a capped SOL event budget. Households whose usual pre-disaster home is in that area can request help, with routine claims paid promptly after private verification and exceptions reviewed by people. Donors can inspect the SOL reserve, decisions, and confirmed transfers without seeing recipients' identities or documents. The first app serves recipients in Thailand and donors worldwide in Thai and English; other countries are a later expansion.

## Problem and users

Relief often needs to be raised after an emergency. Donors cannot easily see the path from reserve to household, while affected people face slow or unclear eligibility and payment processes. ReliefVue serves (1) households who need timely, flexible cash, including lawful foreign residents and renters with acceptable proof of usual residence; (2) donors who want a traceable use of funds; and (3) ReliefVue operators who must review notices and exceptions. People displaced after an event remain eligible based on their verified home, not their current GPS or download location.

## What is distinct and what is not

The proposed distinction is the **complete sequence**: pre-funded Thailand-wide reserve → documented event evidence and human activation → fixed, capped household entitlement → direct payment → public accounting. The normal evidence path uses an original official announcement; an independent path can activate capped household payouts after stronger corroboration and a documented human decision, even before a declaration. The exact evidence threshold remains unresolved. Solana donation and transaction transparency alone are already claimed by other projects, including Sahara and SolanaAid. Chain records prove transfers, not real-world identity, residence, need, or how a recipient spends money. Geraldine intends to authorize disaster events, while a future program-controlled reserve constrains spending to published payout rules; the separate organization wallet holds only labeled operating support. ReliefVue must not claim decentralized governance or protection from misuse until this is built and independently checked.

## Product rules chosen in the brainstorm

- One Thailand-wide SOL reserve; donors can contribute from anywhere. No province or disaster-type earmarking in the first version. Show balances, allocations, payouts, and remainder in SOL, without a fluctuating baht estimate on the public dashboard.
- For the normal route, an original national or local government source defines the affected area. AI may notify and extract areas, dates, and uncertainty; Geraldine checks the source and authorizes activation. Use the smallest defensible area. For a serious event before declaration, ReliefVue may independently activate capped household payouts after stronger corroboration and a documented human decision. Its exact evidence threshold, area boundary method, and emergency cap remain to be decided. A user report or AI verdict alone is insufficient.
- Geraldine approves an event budget and one published household-grant benchmark in Thai baht for that event, regardless of household size. The working basis is the local Thai price of three days of emergency essentials for a standard reference household of roughly 3–5 people; the exact basket, standard size, baht amount, exchange-rate source/time, and SOL disbursement rule remain to be validated. SOL is the reserve and public-accounting currency. If the amount of SOL sent is set using a rate near each payout, households can receive different SOL quantities while targeting the same baht benchmark; a strict SOL event cap still applies. Household size is not a claim field needed to calculate payment, and no household-size supplement is planned for the first policy. The grant contributes toward emergency needs; it cannot promise full three-day coverage for every household. The benchmark is set before claims are paid and is never calculated by dividing the reserve among claimants. Routine claims can be paid automatically inside the cap after private checks, without per-claim founder approval. An exhausted budget creates a queue, not a smaller baht benchmark or a silent overspend.
- For a pre-declaration flood event, the first signed authorization opens only a limited initial tranche of that event budget. Expanding the event budget requires a fresh approval by an authorized reviewer and Geraldine against the updated event terms. The fixed household amount does not change based on the number of claimants; verified claims beyond the available tranche wait for a new authorization or funding.
- Eligibility is one payout per verified household per event, based on its usual home in the approved area when the disaster occurred. Individual damage or neighborhood wealth is not a routine eligibility test. Pre-registration accelerates checks but gives no reserved priority; new registration remains open after an event. Address changes after the announcement and conflicting evidence go to human review.
- **Shared-address household rule (2026-10-04):** A postal address, building, or house may contain multiple households. Separately rented rooms or independently maintained living arrangements can each qualify for one grant after private review; different applicants, IDs, or wallets alone do not prove separate households. Privately record the unit/room when available, a designated claimant, and proportionate residence/tenancy evidence such as a room lease, rent receipt, or other corroboration. Route absent room identifiers, shared bills, disputed occupancy, and apparent duplicate claimants to a human reviewer with an appeal path. Do not publish the room, household composition, or evidence. Thailand's National Statistical Office describes a household by shared living essentials and notes that each room in an apartment/dormitory may count as one household ([NSO household definition](https://www.nso.go.th/public/e-book/Analytical-Reports/Foundation_household_66/19/)); this is a policy reference, not an automated eligibility verdict.
- **No-paperwork route (2026-10-04):** Missing a separate lease, rent receipt, or utility bill sends a shared-address claimant to human review, not automatic rejection. The reviewer may consider alternative evidence of usual pre-disaster residence and a distinct living arrangement, document the reason for approval or denial, and provide an appeal route. Acceptable evidence types and anti-collusion checks still need a tested policy; two reviewers are the initial funded staffing floor, not a promise that they can personally process every claim during a surge.
- **Thai rent-receipt example (2026-10-04):** Geraldine notes that room renters may receive a carbon-copy monthly rent receipt (ใบเสร็จรับเงินค่าเช่า) even without a written lease. A receipt showing the tenant, payment month/date, building, and specific room or sub-address such as 1/1 versus 1/2 can support pre-disaster residence and distinguish units within one building. Review privately against the event date and other available evidence; a name, room number, or receipt alone does not establish household uniqueness or authenticity. Do not assume all renters have receipts or that every 1/1-style label is an official house number. Thai government materials use rent receipts as evidence of rent paid ([government service guide](https://info.go.th/citizen-guides/95ffd391-4187-4b08-a89e-ca50467268f4/export-pdf?procedure_id=95ffd33a-8c1f-4e9e-b8d4-efd662744d00)); this does not make a receipt conclusive proof for ReliefVue.
- **Locked same-room duplicate handling (2026-10-04):** If two applicants claim the same privately identified room/household for the same event, hold the pending claims for human duplicate review. Approve at most one household grant for that event. The first applicant who submitted a complete claim and passes identity, pre-disaster residence, household-uniqueness, and payout checks receives it; a mere first click or incomplete form creates no priority. ReliefVue does not arbitrate family disagreements or require the reviewer to choose a preferred household member. A later applicant does not create a second entitlement; review concrete evidence that the first claimant was ineligible or that the claims actually concern different households, with an appeal path. Keep applicants' private records separate. A room identifier is a practical duplicate signal, not proof that the room had the same occupants across months.
- **AI-assisted duplicate queue (2026-10-04):** Group likely duplicate applications for the same event using private identity, normalized address, room/unit, and supporting evidence so a human reviewer can compare them. Exact matches and rules should be visible to the reviewer; AI can flag ambiguous records but cannot reject a claim. A shared building or street address alone is not a duplicate household, and a match is initially marked "Needs duplicate review." After the reviewer confirms that the same verified household already received or is approved for that event, reject the extra claim with a privacy-safe reason such as "A grant has already been approved for this verified household and disaster event." Record the decision and offer an appeal; do not identify the other claimant or reveal private evidence. If the claims concern distinct households, continue both eligibility checks.
- **Fraud response intent (2026-10-04):** A duplicate match, household disagreement, or erroneous approval is not itself proof of intentional fraud. A human investigates, fact-checks, documents the evidence and decision, privately notifies the affected person, and offers reconsideration. Pause further payment on the disputed claim during review, without an automatic account ban or public accusation. If deliberate deception is substantiated, a time-limited restriction on that person's future claims may be considered by a separate authorized human decision, with an end date and appeal. Do not penalize other household members or treat a wallet/account ban as proof of person-level fraud. A 3–6 month period is only an idea, not a settled ReliefVue rule: excluding someone from emergency essentials in a later disaster may be disproportionate. Define the evidence standard, independent decision authority, permissible restriction, and review schedule before a real-money pilot. UNHCR's fraud guidelines favor proportionate, time-limited measures with reconsideration and warn against withholding essential assistance as a sanction ([guidance](https://emergency.unhcr.org/sites/default/files/UNHCR,%20Operational%20Guidelines%20on%20Addressing%20Fraud%20by%20Persons%20of%20Concerns.pdf)); ReliefVue must design its own policy rather than claim that UNHCR has endorsed one.
- **One claimant account per verified person (2026-10-04):** ReliefVue should state this rule at registration. If identity checks and human review confirm that one person created two claimant accounts, close the extra account, cancel its pending duplicate claim, and preserve the legitimate account and its records. Give private notice, a way to regain access if the second account was created after losing a phone/login, and an appeal if the match was wrong. A name, address, device, or wallet match alone is insufficient to close an account; an allegation of two accounts is not proof of two households or intentional fraud. Account closure for a confirmed duplicate is the baseline policy consequence even if accidental. A separate 3–6 month person-level claim restriction requires a substantiated deliberate-abuse decision and remains open. Wise uses one personal account per person and closes confirmed duplicates, including accidental ones ([Wise account policy](https://wise.com/help/articles/2932130/can-i-have-more-than-one-wise-account), [duplicate handling](https://wise.com/help/articles/2g1s7IE9PgAcrPC0ZKeokg/what-happens-if-i-have-a-duplicate-account)); ReliefVue must adapt the rule to emergency-aid access rather than imply Wise endorses this policy.
- Identity, residence, and household uniqueness are distinct checks. Acceptable evidence may include official residence records, leases, bills, and other corroboration. A bill or TM30 alone is not conclusive. Real documents stay off-chain and out of the public app. Reviewers handle exceptions and appeals with limited private-data access.
- Pay ready, verified households in approval order while funds last. If a household is approved but lacks a receiving method, reserve its payout for a proposed 72 hours; expiry releases the reservation to the next ready household without ending the first household's eligibility. The duration requires pilot testing. A failure of ReliefVue's future baht-conversion provider is a separate service outage, not a household's failure to choose a method: preserve the approved household's queue position and earmarked payout, pause its reservation timer for the verified outage, and resume it after service returns with clear notice. The long-outage contingency and limits on earmarked funds still need design.
- Recipients may choose to keep a SOL payout in their Solana wallet. A future option may let them choose automatic SOL-to-baht conversion and baht delivery through an appropriately authorized provider. Save the recipient's explicit choice as a preference for later events and let them change it before a payout is processed; a later preference change cannot reverse an already completed payout. The final route, quote, fees, and destination must be clear before confirmation. Sending SOL to a wallet does not itself deposit baht in a bank, and a wallet cannot be silently debited for conversion after receipt. The working Devnet demo uses SOL wallets only. In-app wallet creation, real exchange quotes, fiat conversion, and bank delivery are future capabilities, not current claims. People may apply before choosing a receiving method. Recipients decide how to use their aid; those who keep SOL bear later SOL/baht price changes.
- **Closed-shop policy (2026-10-04):** Nearby flooded or closed shops do not by themselves pause an otherwise eligible household grant. Send the approved payment when the household passes the normal checks so it can be used when safe access to goods or services returns, or for another urgent need. Do not imply that cash itself restores local stock, transport, connectivity, or immediate food access; never encourage unsafe travel through floodwater to spend it. Record market-access problems for human monitoring and future relief-partner coordination, without making such a partner a prerequisite for the app-first MVP.
- Public Fund Activity shows proposed, submitted, and confirmed movements separately; only confirmed transfers count as paid. Show amount, purpose, event, status, time, shortened wallet address, and Explorer link where a signature exists. Full addresses remain inspectable on-chain, so call recipients pseudonymous, never anonymous. Never publish names, home addresses, IDs, document images, or a public mapping from household to wallet. Disclose operating and exceptional transfers as well as household payouts.
- Relief and ReliefVue operating funds are separate destinations with separate receipts. The first concept is optional support ($1, $2, or custom value quoted in SOL at confirmation) plus conditional early grants/sponsors. No secured sponsor, tip conversion, fee level, or sustainable salary budget is established. A mandatory fee remains a later decision after measuring operating cost.

## Ideathon MVP: prove the central claim

Use a fictional, clearly labeled Thailand event and synthetic households. A judge without a presenter should be able to follow one narrative: see a funded Devnet reserve; inspect a supplied mock official notice; see AI-assisted extraction only if a real model is configured, otherwise a labeled text scan; perform human event approval; view the exact affected area, payout rule, and event budget; complete one genuine Devnet wallet donation and one genuine eligible household payout; attempt a repeat claim and see it rejected after reload; view signatures and reconciled confirmed totals. Include an outside-area household and a recent-address-change case sent to human review. Show proposed versus confirmed states. Do not collect actual personal documents.

Current deployment gap: the public Vercel build reads a Devnet balance and can verify wallet-signed donations, but its event activation is browser-local, its receipt list is browser-local, and its recipient payout and duplicate rejection are previews. The local demo server can issue real Devnet payouts and persist duplicate state, but is not deployed; its enforcement is backend, not an on-chain program. The public prototype therefore does **not** yet prove the end-to-end pitch.

## Measurable success criteria

For the ideathon: a judge can discover Donate, Request Relief, and Fund Activity without instruction; a real donation and a real payout each have a confirmed Devnet signature; the same synthetic household cannot claim twice for the event after reload; an outside-area profile is blocked and an ambiguous address is routed to review; displayed accounting matches confirmed chain activity; no personal data or secret key appears in the public client; a public explanation distinguishes implemented checks from planned verification and fiat delivery.

For a funded pilot, proposed measures are full claim-to-payment time, payout success rate, unresolved review queue, incorrect approval/rejection rate found through appeals, cost per verified payout, donor understanding of fund allocation, and operating income versus cost. **Locked timing definition (2026-10-04):** Measure from the timestamp when a household submits a complete claim to confirmed payment to that household; include identity, residence, duplicate, and any human review time. Do not restart the clock when a claim moves between queues. Geraldine rejects a 30–60 minute payout promise and proposes 12–24 hours only as a pilot planning window to test, not an observed speed or public guarantee. Define what counts as a complete claim and a confirmed payment for each payout method, plus coverage hours and exceptional-case reporting, before setting a service target.

Scale clarification from Geraldine: 100,000 affected households was an illustrative scenario for why routine claims cannot all require manual approval. It is **not** a forecast, target, known count, or expected size of a city or province disaster. The actual number depends on the event and approved affected area; capacity planning must test a range of claim volumes.

## Open decisions and risks

### Forge: reserve custody and founder authority

- **Locked intent (2026-10-04):** Donated relief funds belong to the public reserve, not Geraldine's personal wallet. Geraldine wants authority to approve an officially supported disaster event, without a personal right to withdraw reserve funds directly.
- **Locked split (2026-10-04):** Donors send relief donations directly to a program-controlled public reserve; a separate ReliefVue organization wallet receives only clearly labeled operating support. Geraldine's authority is limited to approving an event, subject to the reserve's payout rules. This is a future custody design and Devnet demo target, not a claim that the deployed prototype enforces it.
- **Crack:** An ordinary company wallet controlled by one key would still allow its key holder (or an attacker who obtains it) to transfer funds without the app's approval. A secure backend helps protect keys and check claims, but is not itself an enforceable limit on that wallet.
- **Unresolved implementation:** Define the program's payout limits, how private household approvals enter it, and how program upgrades, emergency recovery, and exceptional transfers are governed before real funds are held. Demonstrate with Devnet funds only in the ideathon.

### Forge: competing ReliefVue Network idea

- **Proposal under test, not a pivot:** Add a disaster evidence network that aggregates human reports, sensors, and official alerts, uses AI to review them, records a disaster status on Solana, and offers that status to relief, insurance, logistics, and other applications.
- **Stage correction (2026-10-04):** ReliefVue is a same-day ideathon concept with no users, reserve, investors, or operating partners yet. Their absence is the starting condition, not evidence of failure. Claims about demand, response time, and trust remain hypotheses to test.
- **Locked independent route (2026-10-04):** Before any official alert, a person may submit a private possible-disaster tip with location and in-app photo/video capture. AI may collect corroborating sources and flag it for human fact-finding; the tip itself remains unverified. An original official area alert opens the normal affected-area reporting route. If serious conditions are independently corroborated first, ReliefVue may approve a defined area and capped household payouts before the official declaration, with a documented human decision and the normal private household checks. No individual report or AI verdict automatically releases funds. The exact evidence threshold and emergency cap are still open.
- **Locked flood safeguard (2026-10-04):** Resident reports alone cannot activate a pre-declaration flood payout. Require at least one corroborating source independent of those reports and documented human approval. A source's relationship to the reported location, observation time, and what it actually proves must be checked; rainfall alone, for example, does not prove a household is flooded. Suitable source classes and the remaining threshold are still to be chosen.
- **Locked source interpretation (2026-10-04):** Public measurements or mapped observations published by a government agency can count as independent corroboration even without a formal emergency declaration. ReliefVue remains independent in deciding whether to activate its own event. A reviewer must check source authenticity, timestamp, geographic coverage, and whether the observation supports actual flooding rather than only flood risk or heavy rain.
- **Locked review chain (2026-10-04):** For a future real-money independent flood activation before an official declaration, AI first triages reports and gathers possible corroboration. A separate human reviewer checks source authenticity, observation time, proposed affected boundary, and conflicting evidence, then sends a documented recommendation to Geraldine for final event approval. The reviewer has no reserve withdrawal or payout authority. No reviewer or staff exists yet; use labeled simulated roles for the Devnet ideathon demo.
- **Locked independent-event authorization (2026-10-04):** Before real funds can be released for a pre-declaration event, the future reserve program must require both the authorized evidence reviewer's cryptographic approval and Geraldine's final approval of the *same* event parameters, including affected-area commitment, household amount, and event budget. Neither key alone can activate the event, and the reviewer's key cannot directly withdraw or pay funds. This is a proposed future safeguard, not implemented in the current public app or Devnet backend. Specify signer replacement, key recovery, and program upgrade authority before a funded pilot; a unilateral upgrade path could bypass this rule.
- **Locked staffing intent (2026-10-04):** If funded, plan for at least two coordinating reviewers after the stronger MVP is built and before real claims and reserve operations begin. They can divide routine evidence and claim-verification tasks. For an independent pre-declaration event, either one authorized reviewer plus Geraldine can sign the same event parameters; the second reviewer can handle parallel work and act as backup. Conflicting evidence or a disputed boundary should escalate to both reviewers for assessment before Geraldine's decision. The event-signature rule does not imply that each household claim needs Geraldine's approval.
- **Locked first-tranche policy (2026-10-04):** A pre-declaration flood activation authorizes a limited first event tranche. A later expansion requires another reviewer-plus-Geraldine authorization and updated evidence. Never compute the household payout by dividing the reserve or tranche by the number of claimants. The initial cap formula and household amount remain open.
- **Locked payout purpose and household rule, open value (2026-10-04):** Use a proposed three-day emergency essentials basket priced in Thailand for a standard reference household of roughly 3–5 people to set **one flat baht-value benchmark for every eligible household**, including smaller and larger households. Do not use individual salaries, divide the donation pot among claimants, ask household size to vary the amount, or add a size-based supplement in the first policy. The 3–5-person reference does not exclude larger households. Price safe drinking water, practical ready-to-eat or cookable food, basic hygiene, and other locally necessary items, allowing recipients to choose their actual purchases. Rice and water alone are too narrow, particularly if cooking or shops are unavailable. Describe the grant as a contribution toward three days of essentials, not guaranteed full coverage for every family. Identity/KYC, usual residence, and household uniqueness require separate private checks. Thailand's 2024 National Statistical Office survey reports average monthly household spending of 8,005 baht on food/beverages/tobacco and 22,282 baht overall; those national historical averages are context, not a ReliefVue payout or current affected-area prices ([NSO 2024 survey summary](https://www.nso.go.th/nsoweb/storage/survey_detail/2026/20260128091234_49296.pdf)). The [Department of Internal Trade price site](https://www.dit.go.th/) is a candidate source of Thai retail-price context, but affected-area prices, market access, and item availability still need checking. Cash aid depends on functioning markets and payments ([CALP cash design guidance](https://www.calpnetwork.org/cash-and-voucher-assistance/cva-design-and-delivery/)). The exact baht basket, SOL conversion point, and event cap formula are unresolved.
- **Locked currency distinction (2026-10-04):** Donations, the public reserve balance, event cap, and on-chain transfer accounting stay in SOL. The uniform household grant is priced against a Thai-baht essentials benchmark, rather than a number of SOL whose purchasing value may drift. A recipient who keeps SOL retains the asset and its later price risk; an actual baht payout or conversion option requires a qualified provider, exchange quote, settlement, and separate confirmation. The current Devnet prototype does not offer that option. The rate source, quote timing, fees, on-chain enforcement of variable SOL disbursements under the SOL cap, and treatment of a quote that exceeds the remaining cap are unresolved. Thai digital-asset exchange/broker/dealer activity is regulated; obtain a Thailand-specific legal and provider review before offering conversion ([Thai SEC](https://www.sec.or.th/EN/pages/lawandregulations/digitalassetbusiness.aspx)).
- **Locked payout-choice intent (2026-10-04):** Let a recipient explicitly choose "keep SOL" or, once a qualified provider exists, "automatically convert to baht." Remember that preference for future disaster claims so the recipient does not have to set it repeatedly, while allowing changes before each payout is finalized. Show the selected route and final quote/fees for confirmation. If the selected baht route is unavailable, hold the approved payout, tell the household, and offer a switch to SOL only by explicit choice; never silently substitute payment currency or cancel eligibility. Protect the household's place in the queue and pause its reservation timer during a verified provider outage; no emergency-time pressure to change payment method. The exact default before the first choice, provider integration, consent and key-custody model, and long-outage funding contingency remain open. In the Devnet ideathon demo, present real SOL payout only and label baht conversion as planned.
- **Locked long-outage oversight (2026-10-04):** If the baht provider remains unavailable for days, a human operator reviews the incident, the queue, earmarked SOL, communication to households, and safe recovery options. Human review does not silently change a household's chosen currency, erase eligibility, or approve an uncapped reserve transfer. Routine verified claims on an available payout route may continue under the published event rules without individual founder signatures; "human in the loop" applies to event activation, exceptions, appeals, and service failures. Define review cadence and any long-outage reserve limit before a real-money pilot.
- **Capture intent:** Prefer in-app camera recording rather than a gallery upload for disaster reports. This improves the submission flow but cannot prove when or where the scene occurred, that the device camera is genuine, or that the reporter is independent. Browser file-input `capture` does not reliably prohibit selecting an existing file; a web demo would need direct camera APIs and still label the report unverified.
- **Crack:** An on-chain record proves that a status was published, not that a flood happened, the photo is genuine, or the severity and affected boundary are correct. Multiple reports can come from one attacker; simulated sensors and an AI verdict do not validate the proposed network.
- **Crack:** Independent activation is a meaningful alternative to delayed official action, but it moves responsibility for false positives, false negatives, affected-area boundaries, and wrongful payments onto ReliefVue. Insurance and logistics each require separate data standards, liability models, and operational trust; a simulated demo would not establish those future use cases.
- **Open direction:** Decide whether ReliefVue should remain a household-relief product with evidence-assisted event review, or pursue a separate disaster-data service with one named external consumer and a defensible verification method. Do not claim a decentralized disaster record while one operator controls source selection, AI thresholds, and publication.

### Forge: first hazard and later expansion

- **Locked MVP scope (2026-10-04):** Demonstrate the independent activation path for flooding only, using a fictional Thailand event and synthetic households. This keeps one evidence and payout policy testable within the ideathon. Landslides and tropical-cyclone impacts remain intended later hazards with separate verification rules; they are not included in the independent-activation demo.
- **Cyclone distinction:** Official forecasts can support preparation before landfall, but a forecast track alone does not establish which households suffered harm. The current relief entitlement is tied to verified affected areas and households after local impact. A future pre-impact cash policy would need its own explicit forecast trigger, eligibility rule, and budget; it must not be implied by the flood demo.
- **Landslide distinction:** A landslide can be sudden and highly localized. It needs separate source, boundary, and safety rules; residents should not be encouraged to record dangerous scenes to qualify for aid.

- Exact event-source acceptance and boundary dispute procedure; how to handle a broad or contradictory announcement.
- Who verifies identity, lawful residence, and household uniqueness at real scale; evidence alternatives for people without bills or formal addresses; appeals and reviewer staffing.
- Founder-only treasury authority, compromised-key recovery, exceptional transfers, and independent accountability. Public visibility does not prevent a bad transfer.
- Define the authorized backup-reviewer selection and replacement rule so absence or lost keys do not indefinitely block urgent relief, without letting Geraldine unilaterally appoint a compliant signer during an event.
- SOL value volatility versus the Thai-baht household benchmark; exchange-rate source, quote timing, max SOL per payout, reserve sufficiency, and whether to add a stable-value reserve component before real deployment.
- Secure in-app wallet creation and recovery; fiat conversion/provider, fees, settlement timing, and country-specific compliance.
- Review capacity, 72-hour reservation fairness, offline access, and the financial sustainability of optional support.
- Brand, palette, and visual direction are a separate design decision after the product and proof path are settled.
